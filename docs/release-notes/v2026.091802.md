# The password generator, rebuilt

This release is almost entirely one tool. Heimdall's password generator was a
small thing in the toolbox: a length, some checkboxes, a password. It now does
what a generator people actually rely on has to do, and, just as importantly, it
stopped saying things that were not true.

## It keeps its promises now

Ticking `Digits (0-9)` put digits in the bag the password was drawn from. It did
not put one in the password. With all four kinds ticked and eight characters
asked for, two passwords in five carried no digit at all, and one in two was
missing one of the four kinds. At the default twenty-four it was still one in
sixteen. A site that requires a digit refused them, and nothing on screen had
suggested it might.

The password is now drawn again until every ticked kind is in it. That is a fair
draw among the passwords that keep the promise, rather than a repair of one that
does not, which is why the strength figure can still be stated exactly: it drops
by the share of draws the promise rules out, 1.09 bits at eight characters and
0.10 at twenty-four. When a length is too short to hold one of every ticked kind,
the password is an ordinary draw and the tool says so instead of pretending.

In syllable mode, the length was a length before the rest. Sixteen characters
with two digits, one special and a separator produced twenty-four. A digit is a
character of the password, not an addition to it, and so is a separator: all
three are now paid for out of the length, and sixteen means sixteen. When the
counts ask for more characters than the length holds, the counts give way, the
password stays the size it was asked for, and the notice line says what fitted.

## It says what it is assuming

`Crack time: 243 days` was one scenario presented as a fact. The same password
stands for millennia against a hash deliberately made slow to compute, five
orders of magnitude further down, and the line gave no way to tell which number
you were reading. The attack is now named under the figure: average case, at
10^10 guesses per second against a stolen hash.

The four passphrase word lists are not the same size, so the same four words are
worth 47 bits in English and 38 in Spanish. The strength figure always counted
that correctly and the minimum strength always enforced it, but neither said why
one language needed a longer passphrase than another. The language box now says
how many words it draws from and what one draw is worth.

## There is a great deal more of it

- **A fourth mode, leet**, which rewrites a word and adds digits and specials.
  The substitution table is public knowledge and the word can be typed rather
  than drawn, so neither earns any entropy, and the figure says so.
- **Larger word lists**: English went from 515 words to 3525 and French from 507
  to 2774, which is nearly three bits a word. Latin joins English, French and
  Spanish as a fourth language.
- **A minimum strength** the generator will not go under. It decides the size
  this generation runs at without ever writing your sliders, and says what it
  decided; when even the maximum cannot carry the floor, it says that too.
- **One click, as many passwords as you need**, from one to twenty, with the
  list maskable, copyable as a block and exportable.
- **A case pattern, block by block**, for syllable and passphrase modes, and a
  **placement bar** that puts each digit and each special exactly where you drag
  its cursor.
- **The passphrase gained the controls the other modes already had**: counts of
  digits and specials rather than one of each, the full case box and placement.
- **Specials you can type**: the custom set is held to ASCII punctuation, and the
  clipboard delay is yours to choose rather than fixed.

## The panel

Nine labels were sized in pixels against their English text and clipped their own
translations. They are sized against the longest of the three catalogues now.

The password field announced itself to a screen reader as "Password Generator",
the name of the whole tool. Two buttons announced names that did not carry their
visible label, which is what a voice command needs. All three now say what they
show.

The panel laid forty-odd controls in one column and, in the syllable and leet
modes, ran past the bottom of the window while a third of the width sat empty.
What the tool can tell you about the password it produced moves into a second
column beside the generator. Nothing went behind a tab: a setting that is in
effect but out of sight is how a generator ends up lying to the person using it,
which is the thing this whole release is about. Below 980 pixels of panel the
second column goes back under the generator, because a tool can be opened into a
split pane a third of the screen wide.

## Also

Every button in the tool is drawn by the theme, including one that had been
drawing itself since before the theme existed. The crack time says `1 hour`
rather than `1 hours`, in all three languages and for all six of its units. The
diagram editor speaks Spanish, thanks to a contribution from Nestor (Anghios).

## What this still does not do

It does not check a password against a list of ones already breached. That needs
an outbound network call from a tool that has never made one, and that is a
decision about what Heimdall is, not a feature to slip in.

The Spanish word list is still 725 words against the English list's 3525, so a
Spanish passphrase needs more words to reach the same strength. The tool says so
plainly now, which is the honest half of the fix; the other half wants a Spanish
speaker rather than a guess.
