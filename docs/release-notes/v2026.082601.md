The release that answers a first-time user. Someone who had never seen Heimdall tried it, said
where he got stuck, and everything below came out of walking those paths with him rather than
reading the code. None of it was a bug: every one of these surfaces did exactly what its code
said. They were gaps between what a label promised and what the thing did, and they are only
visible to someone who does not already know the answer.

### One test button, and it says what it does not check

There were two, under two different labels, both sitting inside a credentials card - one of them
directly under the password box. Neither authenticated anything; they opened a TCP socket. Reading
"Test connection" as "check my password" was not a misreading, it was the only reading available.

There is now **one button, beside the address and the port**, with a line under it that says
plainly: it checks that the address and port answer, and does not check your username or password.
Every success message repeats that limit. It works for every protocol that has an address, so
WinRM, Telnet, VNC and FTP gain a test they never had.

**There is deliberately no "test my credentials" button beside it.** The only way to learn that a
password is wrong is to submit it, and every submission is a counted failed logon against a real
account. On SSH the way Heimdall stacks authentication methods makes one press cost two failures,
so a mistyped password can lock the account on the second click under a common lockout policy. A
button that locks you out of the account you are configuring is worse than no button.

### The Domain field says what to type

Its placeholder said "Optional - leave empty to use the domain from the username", and its tooltip
explained the empty case too. Both described what happens when you do **not** fill it in. Neither
said what to write when you do.

It now shows a real example, `CORP or corp.example.com`, and keeps a hint visible after you start
typing - which is where a format mistake actually lives. Whitespace around what you paste is
trimmed. The username hint above it also stopped claiming that Heimdall splits the domain for you;
it does not, deliberately, because keeping the name whole is what makes single sign-on work on
some hosts.

### The SSH username is marked required

It was required to connect and nothing said so. It now carries the same asterisk as Display name
and Server, and refuses to save without one - but only where it is genuinely required. Under
External SSH mode PuTTY asks for the login name itself, so no asterisk appears there.

### A failed connection offers a way forward

The panel you see when a connection fails had two buttons: Reconnect and Close. Reconnect repeats
whatever just failed, so on a wrong username or a wrong port it offered "do it again" and "give
up".

It now offers **Edit profile** and **Copy error** as well. Both already existed - on the Remote
Desktop failure screen only, which is the one you are least likely to meet first.

### The welcome tour points at things

It described the interface instead of showing it: a card in the middle of the screen saying "reach
them from the Tools tab" while you still had to find the Tools tab.

The tour now **highlights the control it is talking about**, dimming everything else, and opens the
right tab before each step rather than after. Six steps instead of three, chosen from where people
actually get stuck, and the last one shows you where to replay the tour.

**You can see it again whenever you want**, from Settings, on the General page. Before, one press
of Escape ended it permanently with nothing anywhere offering to bring it back.

### Projects are gone

Nobody could say how a project differed from a folder, and measured, it did not: it was a coloured
label. It was not a level of the tree, it granted nothing when connecting, and no button anywhere
could create one, so the list was permanently empty. Its one real effect was invisible - "Move to
folder" quietly listed only the folders already used by servers in the same project.

Your existing configuration is read as-is; nothing is discarded.

### Text that was being cut off

A message could lose characters out of the middle of a sentence, silently - no error, no ellipsis,
nothing in a log. Four places, including the title of every message dialog, where a long title ran
off the edge of a window that could not grow.

### Upgrading

Nothing to do. Settings, sessions and gateways are read as they are.
